## CANDIDATE ANALYSIS

### A — THE DIAGONAL

**Silhouette logic:** The classic asymmetric poster pose. Hair mass fills left negative space. Sword creates diagonal counter-line lower-right. This creates the maximum tension between two opposing masses — hair vs blade — with the figure as vertical axis between them.

**Archetype differentiation:**
- vs Zoro: Zoro has human face, three katanas, no helmet. Mikage has sealed ovoid helmet + rectangular slab. Instantly different.
- vs samurai: samurai silhouette is human face + katana curve. Mikage has sensor slits + rectangular slab. Different.
- vs cyberpunk: no glowing parts, no exposed mechanical detail, no busy background clutter. Pure mass.

**Read at thumbnail:** Helmet reads as oval. Slit gaps visible. Sword reads as block (not katana curve). Hair mass left. Pauldrons wider than head.

**Risk:** Sword diagonal angle overlaps with some fantasy warrior poses if proportion is lost. Guard bar is the key differentiator — must be thick and horizontal.

**Recommendation:** Strongest baseline candidate. Highest poster legibility.

---

### B — THE MONOLITH

**Silhouette logic:** Maximum stillness. Sword vertical at right creates a second vertical pillar beside the figure — like two columns. Hair falls straight, adding weight to left. Extremely architectural.

**Archetype differentiation:** No dynamic sword action = not a warrior archetype read. Reads as statue, monument, sentinel — not samurai, not cyberpunk fighter. Distinctive.

**Read at thumbnail:** Clean vertical massing. Sword pillar clearly separate from body pillar. Helmet reads at top. Hair mass left-column. Pauldrons wide horizontal bridge connecting everything.

**Risk:** Two vertical masses (body + sword) could read as two separate figures at extreme thumbnail scale. Guard bar bridging the gap between them is critical.

**Recommendation:** Best for album/release art context. Most "sacred stillness" read of the four.

---

### C — THE CARRY

**Silhouette logic:** Sword carried near-horizontal at shoulder height. This creates a massive horizontal mass extending far right of the figure — a unique silhouette element no other archetype has. Counter-diagonal from diagonal sword = strong rhythm.

**Archetype differentiation:** No archetype carries a horizontal rectangular slab at shoulder height. This pose is completely alien to samurai, cyberpunk, and fantasy warrior conventions. Maximum differentiation.

**Read at thumbnail:** Works if slab width reads clearly. Risk: horizontal sword can blend into the pauldron mass at very small scale.

**Risk:** Highest legibility risk at extreme thumbnail. The horizontal sword + right pauldron can merge into one indistinct horizontal bar. Guard bar must remain distinct.

**Recommendation:** Strong for motion/video thumbnail. Not the primary static poster candidate. Pair with A or B for variety.

---

### D — THE PRESENCE

**Silhouette logic:** Tallest helmet variant (rx=54, ry=78 — most elongated). Widest pauldrons (from x=44 to x=356 — 312px total vs body width of 86px). Near-vertical sword. Maximum width:height ratio on pauldrons. Closest to "architectural monument" read.

**Archetype differentiation:** Extreme pauldron width makes shoulder-read unlike any human silhouette. Tall narrow helmet unlike any warrior archetype. Sword close to body = less dynamic but more monumental.

**Read at thumbnail:** Helmet elongation most readable at this variant. Pauldron width most readable. Hair mass widest.

**Risk:** Near-vertical sword can be confused with body mass at thumbnail scale. Sensor slits positioned higher — may be less visible at small scale.

**Recommendation:** Best for poster context requiring maximum authority. Widest negative space between pauldron edges.
